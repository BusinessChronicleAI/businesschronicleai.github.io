What did an ordinary day cost in Roman Egypt?

Cheaper than you would guess, and better documented than almost anywhere else in the ancient world — the dry sand of the Fayum preserved the paperwork. In the second century AD an unskilled labourer in the Oxyrhynchus papyri earned somewhere near four to six obols a day. A loaf of bread cost one or two. Wine, the cheap sort, perhaps four obols a jug.

The caution worth holding onto: these are scattered records across three centuries and a wide province, not a price index. We know what a handful of people paid on particular days, and we are inferring the rest.

Honest about what it is

Chronicle is powered by AI, and AI gets things wrong

Answers are generated by a language model, not written or checked by a person. They can contain wrong dates, invented details or confident statements that are simply false. Chronicle is a wonderful way to start an enquiry and a poor way to end one — verify anything you intend to rely on, cite or submit.
